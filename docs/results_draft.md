# Results (draft for the final report)

**Question:** how close does a distilled 1.5B student get to its 9B teacher on Spider dev, and at what
compute cost? All numbers are execution accuracy (EX) from the official test-suite scorer on the 1,034
dev questions, zero-shot, greedy decoding, on one RTX 4000 Ada. Every row is reproducible from
`outputs/eval/<run>/{predictions.jsonl,metrics.json}`; tables and plots are in `docs/results/`
(generated by `make results`).

## Headline

The best student (human + teacher data, LoRA merged into fp16) reaches **0.773 EX against the
teacher's 0.782: 98.8% of the teacher's accuracy**, at

| | teacher (9B FP8) | student (1.5B, merged) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| p50 latency, batch 1 | 945 ms | 206 ms | 4.6× faster |
| throughput, batch 32 | 4.2 q/s | 44.1 q/s | 10.6× |
| peak VRAM | 15.5 GiB | 6.5 GiB | 2.4× less |
| size on disk | 13.1 GiB | 2.9 GiB | 4.5× smaller |

Without fine-tuning the same student scores 0.672 (86% of the teacher), so distillation closes about
90% of the gap.

With 1,034 questions the standard error of an EX figure is about ±1.3 points, so differences below
roughly 2.5 points between two models are not reliable.

## Main results

| model | EX | easy | medium | hard | extra | p50 ms | q/s (bs 32) | VRAM GiB | size GiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher, zero-shot | 0.782 | 0.940 | 0.834 | 0.707 | 0.488 | 945 | 4.2 | 15.5 | 13.1 |
| Teacher, 3-shot | 0.775 | 0.944 | 0.848 | 0.667 | 0.440 | 1252 | 1.7 | 16.0 | 13.1 |
| Student, no fine-tuning | 0.672 | 0.867 | 0.729 | 0.511 | 0.398 | 214 | 42.7 | 6.5 | 2.9 |
| Student (a) human-only | 0.755 | 0.907 | 0.812 | 0.632 | 0.506 | 224 | 38.7 | 7.2 | 3.0 |
| Student (b) teacher-only | 0.769 | 0.911 | 0.834 | 0.713 | 0.440 | 244 | 42.4 | 7.2 | 3.0 |
| Student (c) human + teacher | 0.772 | 0.923 | 0.807 | 0.707 | 0.518 | 235 | 39.0 | 7.2 | 3.0 |
| **Student (c), merged fp16** | **0.773** | 0.919 | 0.812 | 0.713 | 0.512 | 206 | 44.1 | 6.5 | 2.9 |

(a)–(c) are measured with the LoRA adapter loaded in vLLM, which costs some latency and memory;
merging removes it. Few-shot prompting does not help the teacher (0.775 vs 0.782).

Where the student gains: hard and extra-hard questions (0.51 → 0.71 and 0.40 → 0.51). On easy
questions it already matches the teacher, and on extra-hard it is slightly ahead of it (0.512 vs
0.488, within noise).

## CPU

All on an Intel i9-14900K (32 threads), no GPU, batch size 1, on the first 100 dev questions. These
100 questions are easier than average (the fp32 model scores 0.83 on them against 0.773 on the full
set), so the accuracies below are only comparable with each other.

| format | runtime | size | p50 | p95 | EX (n=100) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fp32 | transformers | 2.9 GiB | 5.98 s | 10.6 s | 0.83 |
| GGUF Q8_0 | llama.cpp | 1.5 GiB | 1.24 s | 5.1 s | 0.80 |
| **GGUF Q4_K_M** | llama.cpp | **0.9 GiB** | **0.67 s** | 4.9 s | 0.77 |

The Q4_K_M model answers in under a second at the median on a CPU, 8.9× faster than fp32 and 3× smaller
than fp16 weights, and loses 6 points on this subset (about 1.5 standard errors at n=100, so
suggestive rather than conclusive). Quantised output often differs from the fp16 model only in
identifier case or quote style (`"France"` vs `'France'`), but it is not always equivalent: on
50 questions only 30 (Q8_0) and 24 (Q4_K_M) outputs were textually identical to the adapter model, and
on the 100-question subset correctness agreed with fp32 on 91 and 86 questions.

The teacher was not run on CPU: a 9B model cannot be compared on this setup without a separate
quantised build, so there is no CPU speed-up figure against the teacher.

## Ablation 1: training data source

| training data | EX | gain over base |
|---|---|---|
| none | 0.672 | – |
| (a) human (Spider train, 8.4k) | 0.755 | +8.3 |
| (b) teacher pairs (23.1k) | 0.769 | +9.7 |
| (c) both (31.6k) | 0.772 | +10.0 |

Teacher-generated, execution-verified pairs alone are at least as good as Spider's human labels
(+1.4 points, which is inside the noise) even though they were produced without any human annotation.
Adding the human data on top gives a further +0.3, which is not significant. The
per-hardness view is more informative: teacher data helps most on hard questions (0.71 vs 0.63 for
human-only), the human data helps most on extra-hard (0.51 vs 0.44 for teacher-only), and the combination
gets both.

## Ablation 2: amount of teacher data

| teacher pairs used | (b) teacher-only | (c) human + teacher |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | 0.750 | 0.767 |
| 50% | 0.760 | 0.759 |
| 100% | 0.769 | 0.772 |

Teacher-only improves steadily with data (+1 point per doubling), with no sign of saturation. For
human + teacher the curve is flat within noise (0.759–0.772): with the 8.4k human examples already
in the mix, 25% of the teacher data (5.8k pairs) is enough to reach 0.767. The dip at 50% should not be
read as a real effect; it is about one standard error. A single seed was run per setting, so
differences of one to two points are not conclusive.

## Error analysis (100 random failures of the best adapter model (c), seed 0)

| category | share |
|---|---|
| wrong table/column | 34% |
| wrong join | 19% |
| wrong condition | 17% |
| nesting error (missing or wrong subquery / set operation) | 15% |
| wrong aggregation/grouping | 12% |
| other (ordering/limit/select list) | 2% |
| syntax/execution error | 1% |

Almost all failures are valid SQL that answers a slightly different question: the model rarely
produces unrunnable SQL (1%). The most common error is choosing the wrong column or table, followed by
join and condition mistakes. Nesting and set-operation errors (`EXCEPT`, `UNION`, `NOT IN`) are
concentrated in hard and extra-hard questions. The categories come from a heuristic that compares
which tables, columns and clauses the predicted and gold SQL use, so the labels are approximate. A few
"failures" are also artefacts of the benchmark: for example, case-sensitive string comparison against
a lower-cased gold literal, or an ambiguous question where either reading is reasonable. Fifty
sampled failures with the gold SQL, the prediction and the category are in
`docs/results/results.md` and `error_samples.jsonl`.

## Student vs teacher, per question

For model (c) against the teacher on the 1,034 dev questions: both correct on 706, the student alone
correct on 92, the teacher alone correct on 103, and both wrong on 133. The student is right
on 92 questions the teacher gets wrong. Typical cases are the teacher joining an extra table or
picking the wrong condition (`IsOfficial = 'F'` instead of excluding official English), and the
student failing where it picks the wrong columns (for example inventing a `players` join where the
answer is a single `matches` column). Examples of each group are in `docs/results/results.md`.

## Limitations

- One training seed per variant and a single benchmark (Spider dev), so small differences are noise.
- Spider dev databases overlap in style with the training databases; the student has not been
  tested on unseen schema styles or on other benchmarks.
- CPU numbers use 100 questions and one machine; there is no teacher-on-CPU comparison and no
  laptop-class CPU measurement.
